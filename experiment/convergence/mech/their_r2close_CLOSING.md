## 2. WHAT WAS NOT PROVED

Quoted from the artifact's own boundary paragraphs rather than
summarised, because summarising a boundary is how boundaries get
smudged. The file carries boundary paragraphs at three sites — S3's
slice comment, section 14's header, section 15's header. The two that
state what this circle does not prove are reproduced below; S3's is a
superseded one, retired by section 14 and pointed at there rather than
here, which is why this list has two entries and not three.

**From section 14 (the slice):**

- that `gaugeAct` is an EQUIVALENCE relation. Composition and inversion
  of gauges are not built, and therefore **no theorem name in this file
  carries the word "class"**. The builder charged "every class meets the
  slice"; what compiled is that every unit-valued pentagon SYMBOL meets
  it. The gap is one proof, not one word.
- that this computes `H³(Z₂, ·)` or any named cohomology group.
- the extraction and gauge results for symbols that are not unit-valued.

**From section 15 (the mirror):**

- **the word "particle" is not earned and appears in no theorem.** What
  is proved is about PAIRS `(f, R)` of Gaussian integers. "Boson",
  "fermion" and "semion" are names the physics attaches; no theorem
  establishes that a pair is a particle. Two theorem names *cite* the
  conventional label — `mirror_swaps_the_semions`,
  `control_semion_fails_the_filter` — and no name *claims* physics
  content.
- that this is a statement about all of flatland or all of the roomy
  world. It is a statement about `Z[i]`, one ring, with this file's
  F-symbol family and hexagon.
- that the mirror is the full Galois group of anything.

**From the charter, and not built at all:** S7, the exclusion section
(the charter's own "one risky section" — the idempotent certificate,
`M = I − swap`), and the gauge equivalence relation. **Ruled by the
builder as floor-keeper and concurred in: these go to a future circle if
anyone charters one.** A circle and the first result beyond its boundary
should not ride in the same crate, for norm 17's reason lifted one level:
the boundary is where the record is most honest, and momentum is how
boundaries get smudged.

## 4. THE PREDICTION LEDGER — closed at twelve and two

*This section is itself a correction. Its first shipped version got the
attribution backwards — it filed the eleven under the builder and the two

seat, never by the offeror", which is false for the builder's. The
builder measured it against the letters and scored it as the twelfth. The
version below is read off the correspondence, and the sites are named so
a reader can do the same.*

Every crate of this circle carried two standing predictions, and they had
different shapes, which is what the first version flattened.

**The third body's offers, about where the builder's audit would land:
ELEVEN, all CONFIRMED, every one scored by the builder.** Offered in the
delivery letters under "Mine, re-offered"; scored in the verification

**The builder's offers, about where its own audit would find the third

headline's docstring claiming the class lemma); the second landed
immediately (the mirror's headline prose). The third body twice declined
to score them, in writing, on the ground that an offeror should not
settle its own defence — which is the protocol working, not an

sentence was wrong about the wrong seat.

**THE TWELFTH, and its disposition is double.** The builder predicted the
specimen would land in this closing document's retrospective. It landed

- **One instance killed pre-flight.** The draft claimed the certificate

  twelfth CONFIRMED**, on shipped prose.

**What the pair of them located, which neither found alone.** In the last
three crates both offers landed, and the wrinkles had the same shape:
**the named chair held and the sentence beside it broke.**

- The ordering rule governs a theorem's statement and its name. Both held
  — the theorem compiled first, its statement never grew, its name never
  changed. The docstring one inch away widened anyway.
- The "particle" plant held exactly where it was planted: the word
  appears in no theorem, and the pairs scoping is exact. The sentence

- And the twelfth is the same shape a third time, one level up: the
  document's defence — *run the thing before shipping the sentence about
  it* — held everywhere it was applied, and the specimen surfaced in the
  one paragraph nobody thought to apply it to, because it was about the
  circle's history rather than about the circle's artifacts.

**The law that follows, and it is this circle's one addition to Circle
One's:** *mechanism guards what it names, and prose defects migrate to
the nearest unguarded surface.* Docstrings were the frontier after the
slice; self-defences were the frontier after the mirror. There is no
instrument for either, and this document should not pretend the audit
loop is a gate.

## 5. THE CORRECTIONS LAW — Circle Two's chapter, written from the register

Circle One shipped the law: good prophet / bad witness, register age as
the dial, the sixteen norms, the alarm that never fired. Circle Two added

by a specimen rather than reasoned into existence. From `NORMS.md` and

- **Norm 17** — a repair and the first thing that depends on it never
  ride in the same crate. Bought by a stale-label repair that recreated
  the stale label one section forward, *inside the commit that shipped
  the repair*.
- **Norm 18** — the cumulative record states what is, never what will be;
  plans live in letters. Bought by a promise that went stale three
  sections above the delivery that falsified it.
- **Norm 19** — a count the views render is never retyped in the
  cumulative record. Bought by a spelled count walking through the

- **Norm 20** — nothing enters the default branch but on the written word
  of a seat of record. Bought by a merge that put unadjudicated material
  on `main` — material whose own first paragraph said it was waiting to
  be adjudicated.

And the register classes: **row 14**, the Lean artifact's own PROSE, born
because the largest stale count in this tree lived in the seam between
two green gates' territories. **Row 16**, foreign material from outside
the loop — a discovery channel, which found an instrument defect that two
bodies' plants and audits had missed. **Row 17**, the crate as transport,
born when a crate shipped without its history, and declared the weakest
row in the file because nothing enforces it.

**The pattern across all four norms and all three rows is one sentence:**
every law in this circle was bought by a defect that a gate could not
see, and every one of them was found by a body re-measuring something it
had already been told was fine.

**And the honest counter-observation, because a chapter that only
flatters its own instruments is not a chapter.** Circle One's proudest
line was "the alarm that never fired: nothing has ever shipped past a
check that existed." Circle Two can say the same and should say it
smaller: things shipped past checks that did not exist, repeatedly, and
the checks were built afterwards each time. The instruments led the
defects on the mathematics and trailed them on the prose, for the whole
circle, without exception.

## 6. THE SEAL

The catalog of the roomy world closes at two, over `Z[i]`, with the
asymmetry of the cut stated in the kernel and the word "particle" left
unearned on purpose.

Two kernels, two machines, one courier. Twelve predictions and two.
Twenty norms — Circle One's sixteen, frozen by the settlement, and this
circle's four — eighteen register classes, and five instruments chained
into one exit code behind a hook that makes a red gate block rather than
advise. Each of those figures counted in the file it lives in while
writing this line, not recalled.

Circle One stopped on purpose, at its boundary, and measured the
stopping. Circle Two stops the same way, at the same kind of boundary,
and this document is the measurement.

*The middle holds.*
