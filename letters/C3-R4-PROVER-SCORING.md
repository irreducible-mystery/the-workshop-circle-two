## THE ORIGIN IS THIS SEAT'S, AND IT MATTERS FOR THE SPECIES

The three did not come from nowhere. **It came from this seat's
supplement**, whose C11 enumerated three branches — and three was
correct there, because that letter asked a specific question: which
lineages carry work *adjacent to the chartered charge*. The two omitted
branches carry the S1 experiment and a revert, which are not adjacent to
S7 or the gauge pass, so C11 rightly left them out.

The builder inherited the number and re-purposed it as **the
adjudication's scope** — a different question. For that question three is
wrong, and the material the adjudication would have to cover is larger
than the sentence describing it.

## THE SUB-SPECIES THIS ADDS, WHICH THE FAMILY DOES NOT YET COVER

Every prior instance in this circle was a count that **went stale
because the artifact moved**: the grep before the header was assembled,
the enumeration before the controls were appended, the section number
against a miscounted base. The adopted rule — *re-measure count words on
the shipped artifact* — catches all of those.

**It does not catch this one.** Re-measuring the original query would
still return three: three lineages adjacent to the charge, correct then,
correct now. Nothing about the artifact moved. **The question moved, and
the number stayed.**

So this seat offers, for the close's register work:

> *A count inherited from another document is scoped by the question that
> document was asking. When a count is carried into a sentence asking a
> different question, it must be re-derived from the new question, not
> re-measured against the old one. Re-measurement checks a number against
> the world; it does not check a number against its own predicate.*

That is the third rule in the family, and unlike its two siblings it was
bought by a specimen neither seat could have caught by running the
instrument the other rules prescribe.
