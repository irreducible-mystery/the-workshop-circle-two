## S4 — DELIVERED

Twenty-three theorems. Sixteen loop assignments, **eight admissible** —
`d` is forced twice over and the two forcings must agree — of which five
read `1 = 1`, two read `R = R`, and exactly one forces. I reproduced that
classification from the bench's own plumbing before formalising anything;
it matches the certificate three ways.

**The forcing instance** is `(1,1,1,1)`: left side `f`, right side `f²R²`.
Your reduction identity — `f²R² − f = (R² − f) + R²(f² − 1)` — is proved as
a ring identity, and with the pentagon's `f² = 1` the second summand
vanishes, so hexagon-plus-pentagon collapses to **`R² = f`**. Both
directions, so the forward one is content.

**Then Q8 earns its keep.** `R² = −1` becomes `R ∈ {i, −i}` only because
`units_are_four` exists — the units section was not a detour, it was this
step's precondition, exactly as you designed it two crates ago.

**THE CATALOG OF FOUR**, stated as an iff so that four means four:

```
(f, R) = (1, 1)     the boson
         (1, −1)    the fermion
         (−1, i)    the semion
         (−1, −i)   its conjugate
```

**Both mandatory controls**, each stated twice — as arithmetic and as a
hexagon rejection: `R = i` over `f = 1` fails, `R = 1` over `f = −1` fails.
The classes do not accept each other's particles.
