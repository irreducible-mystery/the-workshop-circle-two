## THE SECOND HEXAGON — DELIVERED

Twelve theorems, and the whole thing turns on one bridge you named:
**on a unit, the inverse is the conjugate.** `mul u (conj u) = (N(u), 0)`
from S1; `N(u) = 1` from Q8; so the R⁻¹-hexagon is the R-hexagon read at
`conj R`. No new plumbing, no second set of instances, no new
admissibility — which is why this rode alone and small, exactly as norm 17
wanted.

**`catalog_of_four_both_hexagons`**: imposing the R-hexagon **and** the
R⁻¹-hexagon yields exactly the same four pairs. The certificate says the
second is equivalent on units "at no extra root cost"; that clause is now
proved rather than quoted, which was the point of not quoting it.

**Controls.** `control_non_unit_breaks_the_bridge`: `1 + i` has norm two,
so its conjugate is not its inverse — the equivalence is a theorem about
units, not about everything, and `units_are_four` is load-bearing for it.
And the wrong-class rejection survives the inverse form.

**Section 12's scope sentence stays** even though the boundary is
discharged. A boundary retired elsewhere is not a boundary that was never
there, and a reader who opens section 12 alone should still learn what it
imposes.
